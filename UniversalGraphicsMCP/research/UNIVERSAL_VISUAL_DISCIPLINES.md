# Universal Visual-Creation Domain Map

**Status:** authoritative research baseline for Universal Artist MCP and Universal Graphics/Vision MCP  
**Date:** 10 September 2026  
**Purpose:** map the practical spectrum of visual work, its workflows, deliverables, techniques, tools, constraints, and computational capabilities. “Universal” means an extensible capability system with explicit limitations—not a claim that one model or installation can master every profession without specialist review.

## 1. Research conclusions

Professional visual work is not one task family. It spans expressive art, communication design, spatial design, manufactured-object design, time-based media, scientific/technical communication, and production engineering. The shared process is usually:

1. establish purpose, audience, constraints, rights, budget, medium, and deliverables;
2. research users, subject matter, site, context, precedents, references, and technical constraints;
3. diverge through sketches, thumbnails, studies, prototypes, or simulations;
4. select a direction using explicit criteria;
5. develop an editable model/document/scene;
6. coordinate specialist constraints;
7. test, critique, validate, and revise;
8. preflight, publish/fabricate/build, document, and archive.

AIGA describes design as problem definition, learning, idea generation, design development, and implementation. It also stresses audience, objectives, research, truthful communication, confidentiality, rights, social impact, and environmental responsibility. Architecture adds programming, schematic design, design development, construction documents, procurement, and construction administration. Industrial design adds user research, ergonomics, function, manufacturability, material/finish selection, prototypes, testing, and production collaboration. UX adds behavioral and attitudinal research, information architecture, wireframes, interactive prototypes, usability tests, analytics, and iterative validation. These differences must exist as discipline-specific workflow profiles rather than one generic “make image” workflow.

## 2. Universal capability ontology

Every discipline draws from some combination of these capability families.

- brief interpretation; stakeholder and audience mapping; requirements; constraints; success metrics;
- visual, historical, cultural, market, competitor, precedent, material, site, user, and technical research;

- reference boards, mood boards, visual audits, semantic differentials, taxonomies, and provenance;
- rights, attribution, licensing, consent, privacy, cultural-sensitivity, safety, accessibility, and sustainability checks.

### 2.2 Visual fundamentals
- line, shape, form, mass, space, texture, pattern, scale, proportion, hierarchy, balance, rhythm, contrast, emphasis, unity, variety;
- gesture, contour, construction, perspective, projection, anatomy, drapery, material depiction, light and shadow;
- color appearance, harmony, gamut, adaptation, scene/display spaces, palette, value grouping;
- composition, framing, focal flow, negative space, silhouettes, tangencies, staging, continuity;
- typography, grids, alignment, spacing, legibility, language/script, semantics, and information hierarchy.

### 2.3 Representation
- freehand marks; raster layers; vector paths; text/glyph runs; diagrams; page layouts;
- parametric geometry, CAD/BIM, meshes, curves, surfaces, voxels, point clouds, volumes;
- materials, textures, shaders, lights, cameras, rigs, animation curves, simulations;
- timelines, shots, edits, composites, audio-linked visual events, interactive states;
- geospatial layers, terrain, plant systems, viewsheds, environmental data;

## 3. Fine art and traditional-media workflows

### Drawing
Disciplines include observational, academic, figure, portrait, gesture, botanical, architectural, technical, plein-air, imaginative, caricature, comics, calligraphy, ink, charcoal, graphite, pastel, marker, and mixed-media drawing. Tasks include sighting, proportional block-in, envelope and comparative measurement, gesture, construction, contour, perspective, value organization, edge control, hatching, erasing/lifting, fixative planning, paper selection, scanning, cleanup, colorization, and print reproduction.

### Painting
Oil, acrylic, watercolor, gouache, tempera, ink wash, fresco, airbrush, miniature, mural, decorative, icon, abstract, representational, and mixed media require surface preparation, grounds, underdrawing, value/color studies, underpainting, transparent/opaque passages, glazing, scumbling, wet-into-wet, drybrush, impasto, edge orchestration, drying-aware layering, varnish/display planning, capture, color-managed reproduction, and conservation metadata.

### Printmaking, craft, and physical art
Relief, intaglio, lithography, screen printing, monotype, risograph, letterpress, papercut, collage, mosaic, stained glass, textile art, embroidery, weaving, ceramics, sculpture, installation, and public art introduce separations, trapping, registration, plate/screen constraints, toolpaths, templates, material nesting, firing/shrinkage, armatures, structural attachment, site safety, fabrication drawings, mockups, and documentation. The computer systems can plan, simulate, template, document, and analyze these workflows, but cannot physically execute them without dedicated hardware/people.

## 4. Illustration and visual development

Fields include editorial, advertising, publishing, children’s books, scientific/medical, botanical, fashion, food, technical, courtroom, educational, fantasy/science-fiction, comics, cover, packaging, icon, and concept illustration.

Typical workflow: manuscript/brief analysis → research and rights review → thumbnails → composition/value studies → character/prop/environment development → approved rough → color study → final construction/rendering → correction → reproduction preflight. Deliverables include sketches, layered masters, line art, spot/full-page/spread variants, bleed/crop-safe files, CMYK or spot separations, captions, callouts, accessible descriptions, and rights/provenance records.

Scientific and medical illustration require evidence-based reconstruction, scale/orientation, uncertainty labeling, expert review, and a strict separation between observed data and inferred appearance.

## 5. Concept art, entertainment design, and worldbuilding

Subfields: character, creature, costume, prop, vehicle, weapon, architecture, environment, keyframe, color script, matte painting, story moment, production design, visual development, and world/setting systems.

Work includes narrative and gameplay constraints, shape-language families, silhouette exploration, proportion variants, turnarounds, expression/pose sheets, callouts, material breakdowns, scale figures, kit design, modular sets, location logic, climate/ecology, cultural motifs, technology rules, lighting keys, paintovers, photobashing, 3D blockouts, overpaints, continuity bibles, and handoff to modelers, riggers, animators, level designers, costume/fabrication, and VFX.

A maximal Artist must preserve why decisions were made and maintain consistency across a family of assets—not generate isolated attractive images.

## 6. Graphic and communication design

Subfields include identity, branding, editorial, publication, advertising, campaign, poster, packaging, environmental graphics, signage/wayfinding, data visualization, information design, social media, presentation, motion identity, and design systems.

Professional tasks:
- communication strategy, audience and channel analysis, content inventory, messaging hierarchy;
- naming and visual territory exploration; marks, wordmarks, monograms, symbols, responsive logo systems;
- type selection/custom lettering, color systems, grids, imagery direction, icon families, pattern systems;
- stationery, templates, campaigns, packaging, signage, digital touchpoints, brand guidelines and governance;
- editorial pagination, master pages, styles, folios, tables, indexes, cross-references, prepress and imposition;
- data parsing, truthful encodings, scales, legends, annotation, uncertainty and accessibility;
- production specifications, proofs, separations, bleed, trim, trapping, overprint, spot colors, dielines, finishes, font/image licensing.

AIGA states that branding extends beyond a logo into identity, products, services, and relationships. Typography involves purpose-specific letterforms and systematic families whose variations create contrast while preserving unity.

## 7. Typography, lettering, and type design

Tasks include handwriting/calligraphy, lettering, logotype construction, typeface design, glyph drawing, spacing, kerning, interpolation, masters, variable axes, hinting, OpenType features, Unicode coverage, diacritics, script-specific shaping, proof strings, optical sizes, language testing, font engineering, subsetting, web fonts, licensing, and specimen design.

Graphic typography adds measure, leading, tracking, alignment, rag, justification, widows/orphans, hierarchy, baseline grids, responsive type scales, contrast, accessibility, and output-specific rasterization. The Graphics MCP needs HarfBuzz/Pango/FontTools-class adapters; the Artist owns expressive and communicative decisions.

## 8. Photography and image manipulation

Fields include portrait, fashion, product, food, architecture, interior, landscape, wildlife, sports, documentary, journalism, event, scientific, astro, macro, fine-art, commercial, and computational photography.

Workflow: concept/shot list → location/set/casting/styling → lens/camera/light/color-chart planning → capture and tethering → ingest/checksum/backup → metadata/culling/rating → lens/profile correction → white balance/exposure/tone → local masks → retouching → compositing → color grade → sharpening/noise/grain → proof/export/archive.

Advanced tasks: panorama, HDR, focus stacking, exposure blending, frequency separation, dodge/burn, beauty/product cleanup, perspective correction, channel masking, extraction, relighting, matte creation, restoration, colorization, forensic enhancement with explicit limits, and print soft-proofing. Adobe’s professional workflow distinguishes Lightroom/Camera Raw organization and nondestructive batch/color work from Photoshop’s detailed retouching, selections, layers, and compositing. Color-managed capture-to-print requires calibrated/profiled displays, embedded profiles, rendering intents, printer/paper profiles, gamut awareness, and soft proofs.

## 9. Animation, motion design, film, and cinematics

### Animation
2D hand-drawn, cutout, vector, motion graphics, stop motion, 3D character, technical animation, and experimental animation use scripts, storyboards, animatics, design packs, model sheets, exposure sheets, layouts, key poses, breakdowns, in-betweens, cleanup, ink/paint, rigs, curves, constraints, simulations, lighting, rendering, compositing, sound synchronization, editing, color, QC, and delivery.

Animation principles include timing/spacing, arcs, anticipation, follow-through, overlap, squash/stretch, staging, appeal, exaggeration, pose clarity, weight, secondary action, and continuity. Motion design adds kinetic type, logo systems, transitions, data motion, procedural animation, broadcast packages, projection, and responsive/interactive motion.

### Film and cinematic design
Script breakdown, storyboarding, shot design, lensing, blocking, production design, set/prop/costume coordination, previs, techvis, virtual scouting, virtual cameras, postvis, edit, color, titles, graphics, subtitles, accessibility, masters, and platform versions.

### VFX and virtual production
Plate ingest, lens grids, witness cameras, HDRI, LiDAR/photogrammetry, camera metadata, matchmove, rotoscoping, keying, cleanup, tracking, layout, asset build, texture/lookdev, rigging, animation, FX, crowds, lighting, rendering, deep/multipass compositing, grain/defocus/lens effects, review, conform, color and final QC. VES emphasizes that on-set data, asset integrity and continuity span preproduction through final delivery, and that LiDAR and other captured data can serve multiple departments. Virtual production adds VAD assets, game engines, LED volumes, camera tracking, color pipelines, external lighting, XR, environment capture, and handoff to post.

## 10. 3D art, sculpting, materials, and rendering

Subfields: hard-surface, organic, character, creature, environment, architectural, product, jewelry, CAD-adjacent, stylized, scan cleanup, procedural, motion/VFX, and real-time art.

Tasks include reference/scale setup, blockout, topology, polygon/subdivision/NURBS/voxel modeling, digital sculpting, retopology, UVs/UDIMs, baking, PBR texture authoring, procedural materials, grooming, cloth, rigging, skinning, morphs, animation, cameras, lighting, layout, simulations, rendering, compositing, optimization, LODs, collision, lightmaps, engine export, asset validation, and turntable/presentation.

Professional interchange should cover OpenUSD scene composition, MaterialX materials, OpenColorIO color, OpenEXR renders, glTF/GLB, Alembic, VDB, common mesh formats, and native editable scenes. Formats do not preserve equal semantics, so conversion reports must identify losses.

## 11. Game art and interactive worlds

Disciplines include concept, character, environment, prop, vehicle, weapon, technical art, VFX, animation, UI, lighting, level art, worldbuilding, materials, cinematics, and optimization.

Tasks: modular kits, trim sheets, tileables, decals, vertex painting, terrain, foliage, procedural scattering, destruction, LOD/HLOD, collision, occlusion, draw-call and texture budgets, platform compression, shaders, particles, gameplay readability, navigation-aware dressing, lighting scenarios, baked/realtime GI, profiling, source-control-safe assets, import presets and automated validation.

Technical artists bridge art and engineering with DCC scripts, shaders, procedural tools, engine integrations, rigging, pipeline automation, profiling and build validation. Runtime capture and engine control need explicit Unity/Unreal/Godot adapters rather than generic mouse macros.

## 12. Architecture, BIM, and architectural visualization

Architecture is regulated professional work. The system may assist visualization, drafting, analysis and coordination, but must not certify code compliance, structural safety, accessibility, fire safety, MEP adequacy, cost, or constructability without qualified professionals and jurisdiction-specific authoritative data.

Work spans programming and site analysis; massing and precedent studies; schematic design; design development; coordinated construction documents; visualization; bidding/procurement support; construction administration; record documents and post-occupancy. AIA identifies the traditional phases as schematic design, design development, construction documents, bidding/negotiation and construction, and its schematic checklist includes program, cost, regulation, performance, civil/site, architectural, structural, MEP/fire-protection and consultant coordination.

Visual/technical tasks include zoning envelopes, adjacency diagrams, circulation, plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules, reflected ceiling plans, materials, furniture, daylight/shadow, views, BIM families, clash/coordination visualization, phasing, renderings, diagrams, presentation boards, VR walkthroughs, and drawing-set QA.

Architectural visualization adds CAD/BIM import, model cleanup, materials, landscaping, entourage, sun/sky/HDRI, physically based cameras, stills, animation, virtual tours, photomontage, atmospheric storytelling, and truthful differentiation between proposal and existing conditions.

## 13. Interior, spatial, exhibition, and home design

Interior architecture/design includes programming, survey, adjacency and circulation, space planning, anthropometrics, accessibility, furniture layouts, millwork, finish palettes, lighting, acoustics, reflected ceiling plans, power/data coordination, elevations/details, schedules, cost alternatives, renders, sample boards, procurement and installation documentation.

Related practices include residential styling, kitchen/bath, retail, hospitality, workplace, healthcare, set, exhibition, museum, event, stage, experiential, and environmental graphic design. Tasks include sightlines, visitor flow, narrative sequencing, display systems, graphics, lighting cues, accessibility, wayfinding, durability, maintenance, assembly/disassembly and installation plans.

The Artist can generate options and presentations; the Graphics MCP can construct spatial diagrams and renders; qualified professionals remain responsible for building, electrical, fire, accessibility and structural decisions.

## 14. Landscape, urban, and environmental design

Work includes site inventory, survey/GIS, terrain, hydrology/drainage visualization, soils, climate, solar/wind, ecology/habitat, vegetation, cultural/historic context, circulation, accessibility, grading, planting, hardscape, water, lighting, furniture, viewshed/visual impact, phasing, maintenance, community engagement and construction documents.

ASLA describes site analysis using mapping, surveys, record review and condition assessment—including grading/drainage, erosion, circulation and climate—and emphasizes health, safety, accessibility, maintenance capacity, fiscal/legal/social factors, habitat, biodiversity and historical context. Deliverables include inventories, analyses, concepts, master plans, grading/planting/details, perspective views, sections, seasonal/growth visualizations, public-engagement graphics and management plans.

Urban design adds district/public-realm analysis, morphology, density, mobility, frontage, public-space networks, scenario planning, codes/guidelines, stakeholder communication and large-scale phasing.

## 15. Product and industrial design

Industrial design covers consumer products, furniture, appliances, tools, electronics, mobility, medical products, packaging structures, sports equipment and services/systems.

Workflow: opportunity/user research → requirements → ergonomic and context analysis → divergent sketches → form/CMF studies → CAD/3D models → appearance renders → mockups → functional prototypes → usability/engineering/manufacturing tests → design refinement → specifications/technical drawings → tooling/manufacturer coordination → production/marketing.

IDSA identifies appearance, functionality, manufacturability and end-user value as central, with skills including sketching, 3D modeling/rendering, user research, visual storytelling, rapid prototyping/testing, color-material-finish, engineering/fabrication, programming, manufacturing, marketing and branding. This field proves why Artist and Graphics must cooperate with CAD and simulation but not pretend to replace engineering validation.

## 16. Fashion, textile, costume, and wearable design

Fields include apparel, couture, ready-to-wear, knit, activewear, footwear, accessories, jewelry, costume, surface pattern, textile construction and digital fashion.

Tasks: trend/cultural/market research, concept and color story, fabric/yarn/trim sourcing, fashion illustration, silhouette development, technical flats, textile repeats, weave/knit/print/embroidery planning, draping, blocks/slopers, pattern drafting, seam and construction design, grading, markers, 3D garment simulation, sample/prototype, fitting, corrections, tech packs, costing, production handoff, lookbook, runway/presentation and archival.

CFDA production profiles confirm the practical chain of draping, fittings, pattern making/adjustment, CAD digitization, prototypes, final samples, grading, technical design, tech packs, sourcing and production coordination. The system must preserve measurements, sizes, grain, seam allowance, material behavior, construction callouts and revision lineage.

## 17. Transportation, mobility, marine, aerospace, and vehicle design

Tasks include package/occupant constraints, proportion and stance, exterior/interior ideation, orthographic and perspective sketching, tape drawing, alias-class surface development, ergonomics, HMI, CMF, lighting signatures, aerodynamic/engineering visualization, digital/physical models, VR review, configurators, manufacturing feasibility and presentation animation.

These workflows combine industrial design, transportation styling, Class-A surfacing, visualization, UI, branding and engineering data. Safety, homologation and engineering claims require specialist validation.

## 18. Engineering visualization, technical drawing, and diagrams

Fields: mechanical/electrical/civil/process/software/network/scientific/medical/geospatial visualization; patent drawing; assembly/service manuals; schematics; maps; charts; explanatory animation.

Tasks include orthographic/isometric/axonometric/perspective projection; dimensions/tolerances/symbols; sections/exploded views; parts callouts; line hierarchy; revision blocks; P&IDs; wiring; flowcharts; UML; network topology; maps/GIS; charts; volume/vector/particle visualization; uncertainty; annotation; animation; accessible descriptions; publication/export.

The system needs domain symbol libraries and user-supplied standards. It must never infer safety-critical values, tolerances, ratings or compliance. Diagrams should distinguish topology/meaning from geometry/style so they remain editable and verifiable.

## 19. UI, UX, service, and interaction design

Work includes product strategy, discovery, user research, task/domain modeling, personas when evidence-based, journeys, service blueprints, information architecture, content design, user flows, wireframes, interaction states, prototypes, visual design, design systems, accessibility, usability testing, analytics, experimentation and developer handoff.

NN/g maps research methods across attitudinal/behavioral, qualitative/quantitative and context-of-use dimensions. Methods include interviews, field studies, contextual inquiry, diary studies, participatory design, card sorting, tree testing, concept/desirability testing, prototype/usability tests, analytics and A/B tests. Common deliverables include wireframes, interactive prototypes, flows, site maps, usability reports, style guides and pattern libraries.

Graphics handles assets, layout geometry, screenshots and visual diffs. Artist/Designer owns goals, interaction models, content hierarchy, states, accessibility and research interpretation. Real user research cannot be fabricated by an AI.

## 20. Web, generative, data, immersive, and emerging visual work

Additional fields include creative coding, generative art, computational design, algorithmic typography, data art, projection mapping, installations, interactive exhibits, AR/VR/MR/XR, spatial interfaces, virtual production, digital twins, volumetric video, neural rendering, synthetic data, NFTs/blockchain-based work where lawful, robotics-mediated art, and bio/material experiments.

Capabilities include shaders, particles, agents, L-systems, grammars, fields, reaction-diffusion, fractals, optimization, parametric constraints, live sensors, OSC/MIDI, WebGL/WebGPU, game engines, spatial anchors, stereoscopy, 360 media, hand/eye/body tracking, haptics, real-time audio-reactive systems and multi-display calibration.

These projects require runtime engineering, physical setup and safety review in addition to visual design.

Fonts: OTF, TTF, WOFF/WOFF2, variable fonts with license metadata.

## 24. “Maximum universal” implementation rule

The base install must stay stable and modest. Breadth comes from separately installable capability packs:

1. raster-photo; 2. color-VFX; 3. vector-typography-layout; 4. classical-CV; 5. OCR-document; 6. video-animation; 7. ML-inference; 8. detection-segmentation-tracking; 9. generation-restoration; 10. 3D-USD-materials; 11. CAD-BIM; 12. architecture-landscape-GIS; 13. UI-UX-prototyping; 14. game-engine; 15. creative-app adapters; 16. fabrication/output; 17. Windows screen-analysis integration.

Every pack needs capability descriptors, dependency/license manifests, resource estimates, health checks, sample fixtures, deterministic fallback where possible, timeouts, cancellation, provenance and uninstall/rollback. No model, DCC, driver or huge framework is silently installed.

## 25. Limits and professional responsibility

No finite taxonomy literally covers every future visual practice. “Universal” therefore means discoverable extension points and composable primitives. The systems must not claim expertise or successful validation when a required application, model, device, standard, user study, site visit, material test, proof, fabrication step or licensed professional review did not occur.

Architecture, landscape architecture, engineering, medical communication, accessibility compliance, construction, manufacturing, fire/life safety and similar fields can be safety-critical or regulated. Generated visualizations are assistance and communication—not certification. Local law, contracts, codes, standards and qualified practitioners control.
